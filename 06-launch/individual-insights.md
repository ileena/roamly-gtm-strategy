## 1. Launch plan

| Phase | Goal | Key activities | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Pre-launch** | Prepare a focused pilot and make sure the full group-booking journey is ready to test. | Select a limited set of cities and hosts verified for group capacity; finalize the group booking, invitation, shared itinerary, and split-payment flows; define payment deadlines, cancellation and refund handling; confirm analytics tracking and baseline measurement; prepare in-product and email messaging. | Product, Growth, Host Operations, Payments, Support, Analytics |
| **Launch** | Drive qualified existing users into the Roamly Groups flow and validate whether better coordination improves completed group bookings. | Launch targeted in-product prompts and email to existing users organizing trips; enable organizers to invite friends; monitor group-booking completion and invite-to-payment conversion; test travel-community recommendations and trip stories as a supporting channel; monitor support issues and participant drop-off. | Product, Growth, Support, Host Operations, Analytics |
| **Post-launch** | Evaluate the GTM bet and decide what should be improved or scaled. | Analyze group-booking completion, split-payment adoption, repeat group bookings, incremental platform revenue, cancellations, support contacts, and host fulfillment; investigate drop-off points; collect organizer and participant feedback; refine the experience and expand only if the evidence supports it. | Product, Analytics, Growth, Host Operations, Support |

## 2. Launch metrics & guardrails

- **Success looks like:** Higher group-booking completion, strong split-payment adoption, repeat use of the shared itinerary, and evidence that Roamly Groups generates incremental booking value. Numerical targets should be set after establishing a baseline.

- **Tripwire (when to pull back):** Pull back or pause expansion if the pilot shows that groups are starting the flow but consistently failing to complete bookings, or if cancellations, payment issues, support contacts, or host-fulfillment problems indicate that the group experience is creating more friction than value.

## 3. The full GTM story in one slide

Roamly Groups is built around one clear customer problem: the friend organizing a group trip carries the burden of coordinating plans, bookings, and payments across multiple people and tools.

Our GTM strategy is to expand usage among existing Roamly customers through a product-led group experience, with travel communities as a supporting discovery channel.

Instead of competing with Airbnb and larger marketplaces on inventory size, Roamly Groups competes on group coordination by bringing vetted local experiences, group booking, shared itinerary planning, and split payments into one experience.

This positioning drives the message:

**“Plan the trip together. Without the group-trip chaos.”**

The commercial model follows the same value moment through transaction-based pricing, where travelers pay when participating in a group booking.

The launch begins with a focused pilot using qualified existing users and group-ready hosts. The main measure of the bet is group-booking completion, supported by invite-to-payment conversion, split-payment adoption, repeat group bookings, and incremental platform revenue.

**The GTM bet:** We are betting that groups will choose a better coordination experience over a larger marketplace when planning and paying together becomes difficult.

## 4. Individual insights

*This is the reflection you share. Be honest and specific.*

- **Biggest "aha" across the certification:** My biggest “aha” was realizing that GTM is a chain of connected decisions rather than a set of separate deliverables. Once I clearly defined the target customer and the core problem, the competitive strategy, positioning, messaging, pricing, and launch decisions became much easier to connect.

- **What changed in how you think about GTM:** I used to think about GTM mainly as how a product is launched and promoted. I now see it as a broader strategy that starts with deciding who the product is for, what problem matters most, where the product can realistically win, and then aligning the motion, positioning, messaging, pricing, channels, and metrics around those choices.

- **What you'd do differently with more time / data:** I would validate more of the assumptions before expanding the launch. I would study which types of groups have the highest coordination pain, where participants drop out of the booking journey, how much travelers are willing to pay for the added convenience, and whether group-ready hosts can consistently deliver the experience.

- **The one idea you'll apply at work on Monday:** I will separate what we know from what we are assuming before making a product or GTM decision, identify the riskiest assumption, and define how we will validate it before scaling.
