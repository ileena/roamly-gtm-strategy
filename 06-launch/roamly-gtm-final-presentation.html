Battlecard

Our one-line advantage

Roamly Groups brings group booking, split payments, and shared trip planning into one coordinated experience.

Trap question

How does your group currently coordinate the booking, collect everyone's payment, and keep the trip plan updated?

Objections

“We already use Airbnb.”
Airbnb is strong for booking travel, but Roamly Groups is designed around coordinating the whole group.

“We can just use WhatsApp and send each other money.”
You can, but that means managing the trip across multiple tools. Roamly keeps the group journey in one place.

Strategic bet
We are betting that groups will choose a better coordination experience over a larger marketplace when planning and paying together becomes difficult.
Why now

Planning, booking, communication and payments are still fragmented across different tools.

Not doing

Competing with large travel platforms on inventory size.

90-day proof
  • Higher group-booking completion
  • Strong split-payment adoption
  • Repeat use of shared itinerary planning

06 · Individual Insights

What I learned from building the GTM.

01
Friction points

Narrowing the strategy

The hardest part was narrowing the strategy and making clear choices instead of trying to solve everything at once.

I had to decide whether Roamly Groups should compete on marketplace size or focus on group coordination, and whether pricing should be subscription-based or transaction-based.

I worked through this by consistently going back to the target customer, their main pain point, and the moment where the product creates value.

02
Biggest “aha” moment

GTM is a chain of connected decisions

My biggest “aha” moment was realizing that a strong GTM strategy is a chain of connected decisions.

Once I clearly defined the target customer as the friend organizing the group trip and the core problem as coordination, the positioning, messaging, competitive strategy, and pricing decisions became much easier and more consistent.

03
Key takeaways

GTM starts before launch

GTM is not just about launching or promoting a product. It starts with choosing the right customer and problem and understanding where you can realistically win.

Positioning, messaging, channels, pricing, and success metrics should all align around the same strategy.

I also learned the importance of separating assumptions from validated evidence and testing the riskiest assumptions before scaling.

My biggest learning
Strong GTM is not a collection of separate deliverables — it is a set of connected choices built around the same customer, problem, and value.
